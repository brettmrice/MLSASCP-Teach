Primary Hemostasis Testing

Learning Objectives

  • Correlate automated platelet parameters (platelet count, MPV, PDW) with peripheral blood smear findings to formulate a differential diagnosis for thrombocytopenia and resolve pre-analytical artifacts such as EDTA-dependent pseudothrombocytopenia and platelet satellitism.
  • Explain the operating principle of the Platelet Function Analyzer (PFA-100/200), detail the functions of Collagen/Epinephrine (CEPI) and Collagen/ADP (CADP) cartridges, and interpret closure time patterns to differentiate aspirin effects from intrinsic platelet defects and von Willebrand disease.
  • Differentiate the analytical components of the von Willebrand Disease (vWD) diagnostic panel (vWF:Ag, vWF:Activity, Factor VIII coagulant activity) and utilize the Activity-to-Antigen ratio to classify vWD into Types 1, 2 (subtypes 2A, 2B, 2M, 2N), and 3.
  • Explain the technical methodology, calibration, and agonist mechanisms (ADP, Epinephrine, Collagen, Arachidonic Acid, Ristocetin) of Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA) using platelet-rich and platelet-poor plasma.
  • Interpret characteristic LTA aggregation curves to definitively differentiate Glanzmann thrombasthenia, Bernard-Soulier syndrome, dense granule storage pool disease, and cyclooxygenase (COX-1) inhibition.
  • Formulate a systematic diagnostic reflex testing algorithm for primary hemostasis disorders transitioning from routine screening (CBC, PBS, PFA) to specialized confirmatory testing (vWD panel, LTA, flow cytometry).

Automated CBC Platelet Parameters and Smear Correlation

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Primary hemostasis represents the immediate physiological response to vascular endothelial disruption. The primary components are the vascular intima, platelets (the cellular plugs), and von Willebrand factor (vWF, the molecular bridge). Defects in primary hemostasis produce characteristic mucocutaneous hemorrhage, including petechiae, purpura, ecchymoses, epistaxis, and mucosal oozing.

The initial laboratory evaluation of primary hemostasis begins on automated hematology analyzers using two detection principles:

  • Electrical Impedance (Coulter Principle): Citrated or EDTA whole blood is diluted in an electrically conductive electrolyte solution and drawn through a microscopic aperture flanked by internal and external electrodes. As non-conductive platelets pass through the sensing zone, they increase electrical resistance, generating voltage pulses. The pulse count reflects cell concentration, while pulse amplitude is proportional to cellular volume. Particles sized between 2 and 20 fL are classified as platelets to derive the platelet count, Mean Platelet Volume (MPV), and Platelet Distribution Width (PDW).
  • Optical Light Scatter (Flow Cytometry): Hydrodynamically focused cells traverse a laser beam. Forward Angle Light Scatter (FSC, low angle $1^\circ\text{--}5^\circ$) corresponds to cell volume, whereas Side Scatter (SSC, orthogonal $90^\circ$) measures internal structural complexity and granularity. Platelets exhibit a characteristic low FSC and low SSC profile. Analyzers equipped with fluorescent optical channels utilize polymethine or oxazine dyes to bind intra-platelet RNA, enumerating "reticulated" (young) platelets and resolving cellular fragments from true platelets.

Platelet Parameters and Reference Intervals

  • Platelet Count (PLT): ASCP Standard Reference Range is $150\text{--}450 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ ($150\text{--}450 \times 10^9/\text{L}$).

  • Mean Platelet Volume (MPV): Reference interval is $7\text{--}11\text{ fL}$. Calculated directly from the platelet volume histogram:

    $$\text{MPV (fL)} = \frac{\text{Plateletcrit (PCT %)} \times 1000}{\text{PLT Count } (\times 10^3/\mu\text{L})}$$

  • Platelet Distribution Width (PDW): Reflects platelet volume heterogeneity (anisocytosis), analogous to the red cell RDW. A high PDW signifies simultaneous circulation of newly produced large platelets alongside mature platelets.

Pre-Analytical Artifacts and Troubleshooting

  • EDTA-Dependent Pseudothrombocytopenia: Caused by naturally occurring anti-platelet autoantibodies (typically IgG or IgM) that recognize cryptic epitopes on platelet glycoprotein IIb/IIIa exposed only when EDTA chelates extracellular calcium. In vitro agglutination produces large platelet aggregates. Automated impedance apertures count these aggregates as single leukocytes or reject them, triggering false thrombocytopenia and pseudoleukocytosis.
    • Corrective Action: Recollect blood in a 3.2% sodium citrate tube. Multiply the analyzer platelet count by $1.1$ to adjust for the $9:1$ blood-to-anticoagulant dilution volume.
  • Platelet Satellitism: Platelets adhere to the outer membranes of band and segmented neutrophils, forming complete rosettes. This is mediated by cold-reacting IgG targeting neutrophil Fc$\gamma$RIII receptors and platelet GPIIb/IIIa in the presence of EDTA.
    • Corrective Action: Collect in sodium citrate or perform an immediate fingerstick manual smear without anticoagulant.
  • Interference by Microcytic RBCs / Schistocytes: In microangiopathic hemolytic anemias (MAHA: TTP, HUS, DIC), extreme red cell fragmentation produces schistocytes $< 20\text{ fL}$, falsely elevating impedance platelet counts.
    • Corrective Action: Confirm using optical fluorescent channels or manual hemocytometer counts using $1\text{%}$ ammonium oxalate phase-contrast microscopy.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Never report a critical low automated platelet count ($< 50 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) without performing a peripheral blood smear review to rule out clumps or satellitism.
  • A low platelet count paired with a high MPV indicates peripheral destruction with active bone marrow megakaryopoiesis (e.g., ITP).
  • A low platelet count paired with a low MPV points toward impaired thrombopoiesis, classically seen in Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome or bone marrow aplasia.
  • When recollecting an EDTA clumped sample in a 3.2% sodium citrate tube, the final instrument count must be multiplied by $1.1$ before entering into the laboratory information system (LIS).

Platelet Function Analyzer (PFA-100/200) Screening and Interpretation

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

The Platelet Function Analyzer (PFA-100/PFA-200) serves as an automated, high-shear in vitro screen of primary hemostasis, replacing the obsolete Duke and Ivy bleeding time assays.

Operating Principle

Citrated whole blood ($800\text{ }\mu\text{L}$) is aspirated under constant negative vacuum through a capillary tube and through a microscopic aperture ($147\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ diameter) cut into a nitrocellulose membrane. The membrane is embedded with equine Type I collagen and either epinephrine bitartrate or adenosine diphosphate (ADP).

The physical movement of blood through the micro-aperture generates high hydrodynamic shear rates ($5,000\text{--}6,000\text{ s}^{-1}$), mimicking arteriolar microcirculation. Under these shear conditions, von Willebrand factor unwinds, binding subendothelial collagen via its A1 domain and tethering platelet GPIb/IX/V complexes. Subsequent activation promotes stable binding through activated GPIIb/IIIa integrins, inducing platelet aggregation and degranulation.

As the aggregate enlarges, it physically occludes the $147\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ opening. The elapsed time from blood introduction to total cessation of blood flow is reported in seconds as the Closure Time (CT). If occlusion does not occur before the preset analytical ceiling (typically $300\text{ seconds}$), the instrument reports the result as non-closing ($> 300\text{ seconds}$).

Cartridge Dynamics

  • Collagen/Epinephrine (CEPI) Cartridge: Coated with $2.0\text{ }\mu\text{g}$ equine type I collagen and $10\text{ }\mu\text{g}$ epinephrine bitartrate. Epinephrine is a weak biochemical agonist that relies on cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1) signaling and thromboxane $\text{A}_2$ production. CEPI serves as the initial broad-spectrum screening cartridge; it is sensitive to aspirin, NSAIDs, platelet storage pool defects, severe intrinsic receptor deficiencies, and vWD.
  • Collagen/ADP (CADP) Cartridge: Coated with $2.0\text{ }\mu\text{g}$ equine type I collagen and $50\text{ }\mu\text{g}$ ADP. ADP is a potent platelet agonist that bypasses the COX-1 enzyme by activating $\text{P2Y}_1$, $\text{P2Y}_{12}$, and $\text{P2X}_1$ purinergic receptor pathways directly. The CADP cartridge is tested secondary to a prolonged CEPI to differentiate drug-induced COX-1 inhibition from severe primary hemostatic pathology.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

Pre-Analytical Criteria and Exclusion Rules

Prior to loading a sample into the PFA analyzer, the Medical Laboratory Scientist must verify three parameters:

  1. Minimum Platelet Count Threshold: PLT count must be $\ge 100 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$. If the platelet count is $< 100 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$, there are insufficient platelets to mechanically occlude the aperture, resulting in an artifactually prolonged closure time regardless of functional competency.
  2. Minimum Hematocrit (HCT) Threshold: HCT must be $\ge 30\text{%}$. Erythrocytes provide the rheological force that pushes platelets toward the peripheral endothelial margin (platelet margination). Anemia below $30\text{%}$ HCT impairs this radial displacement, artifactually prolonging closure times.
  3. Specimen Integrity and Stability: Specimen must be whole blood collected in $3.2\text{%}$ sodium citrate ($9:1$ blood-to-anticoagulant ratio). Underfilled tubes cause excess free citrate, altering free ionized calcium levels and falsely prolonging closure times. Testing must be completed within $4\text{ hours}$ of venipuncture; samples must remain at ambient room temperature ($15\text{--}25^\circ\text{C}$). Refrigeration triggers spontaneous shape change and cold activation of platelets, invalidating results.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • PFA is a sensitive screening assay, not a definitive diagnostic test. It confirms or rules out primary hemostasis abnormalities but cannot establish an etiology on its own.
  • Always check the CBC before loading a PFA: if PLT is $< 100 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ or HCT is $< 30\text{%}$, cancel testing and alert the clinical team that closure times will be artifactually prolonged.
  • A prolonged CEPI coupled with a normal CADP is pathognomonic for aspirin or NSAID ingestion within the laboratory evaluation of primary hemostasis.
  • Prolongation of both CEPI and CADP necessitates an immediate reflex to a von Willebrand disease panel, as vWD is the most common inherited cause of this pattern.

Von Willebrand Disease (vWD) Diagnostic Panel Evaluation

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Von Willebrand factor is an ultra-large multimeric glycoprotein synthesized by vascular endothelial cells (stored in Weibel-Palade bodies) and megakaryocytes (stored in platelet $\alpha$-granules). In primary hemostasis, it binds exposed subendothelial collagen via its A3/A1 domains and captures platelets via GPIb$\alpha$ under high vascular shear. In secondary hemostasis, vWF serves as the non-covalent chaperone carrier protein for procoagulant Factor VIII (FVIII), extending the half-life of Factor VIII from 2 hours to 8–12 hours.

The baseline vWD diagnostic panel utilizes a three-component approach:

Panel Methodologies

  • von Willebrand Factor Antigen (vWF:Ag): Quantifies total concentration of circulating vWF protein, independent of functional multimeric structure.
    • Methodology: Automated Latex Immunoassay (LIA). Sub-micron polystyrene latex particles coated with polyclonal rabbit or monoclonal mouse anti-human vWF antibodies agglutinate in the presence of patient plasma vWF. The increase in optical density/turbidity measured spectrophotometrically is directly proportional to vWF:Ag concentration. Reference range: $50\text{--}150\text{ IU/dL}$ ($50\text{--}150\text{%}$).
  • von Willebrand Factor Activity (vWF:Activity): Measures the physiological capacity of vWF to bind the platelet glycoprotein Ib receptor.
    • Classic Method (Ristocetin Cofactor Activity - vWF:RCo): Relies on the antibiotic ristocetin, which alters the conformation of the vWF A1 domain to bind platelet GPIb in vitro. Patient plasma dilutions are mixed with formalin-fixed, normal donor washed reagent platelets and ristocetin. Agglutination velocity is monitored turbidimetrically. Reference range: $50\text{--}150\text{ IU/dL}$.
    • Modern Automated Assays (vWF:GPIbM / vWF:GPIbR): vWF:GPIbM utilizes recombinant, gain-of-function mutated GPIb fragments that spontaneously bind patient vWF without requiring ristocetin, eliminating the high coefficient of variation ($> 15\text{--}20\text{%}$) associated with classical vWF:RCo.
  • Factor VIII Coagulant Activity (FVIII:C): Evaluates the biological activity of Factor VIII using a modified, one-stage clot-based Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT).
    • Methodology: Patient plasma is diluted and combined with commercially prepared Factor VIII-deficient substrate plasma containing all other human coagulation proteins. The degree of clotting time correction when an aPTT activator and calcium chloride are added is compared against a standard curve. Reference range: $50\text{--}150\text{%}$.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

The vWF:Activity to vWF:Ag Ratio

Calculating the Activity-to-Antigen ratio is the central step in differentiating quantitative from qualitative vWD:

$$\text{vWD Ratio} = \frac{\text{vWF:Activity (IU/dL)}}{\text{vWF:Ag (IU/dL)}}$$
  • Ratio $\ge 0.7$: Indicates that any deficiency in vWF activity is proportional to the deficiency in protein mass. This denotes a quantitative defect (Type 1 or Type 3).
  • Ratio $< 0.7$: Indicates that the vWF protein is present in plasma, but its capacity to bind platelets is compromised. This denotes a qualitative defect (Type 2 variants: 2A, 2B, 2M).

Diagnostic Criteria for vWD Classifications

  • Type 1 (Partial Quantitative Deficiency, $70\text{--}80\text{%}$ of cases): Mild-to-moderate decrease in all parameters. vWF:Ag $10\text{--}40\text{ IU/dL}$, vWF:Act $10\text{--}40\text{ IU/dL}$, FVIII:C normal or proportionally decreased. Ratio $\ge 0.7$. Multimer analysis confirms a normal distribution of all multimer sizes, with reduced overall band density.
  • Type 2 (Qualitative Defects, $15\text{--}20\text{%}$ of cases):
    • Type 2A: Mutations in the A2 domain increase susceptibility to ADAMTS13 cleavage, or mutations in the D3 domain impair multimer assembly. Results: Ratio $< 0.7$. Multimer analysis reveals a loss of high- and intermediate-molecular-weight multimers.
    • Type 2B: Gain-of-function mutation in the vWF A1 domain causing spontaneous hyper-affinity binding to resting platelet GPIb. Platelet-vWF aggregates are cleared from circulation, leading to intermittent thrombocytopenia and loss of high-molecular-weight multimers. Characterized by hyper-responsiveness to low concentrations of ristocetin ($0.5\text{ mg/mL}$) in Low-Dose Ristocetin-Induced Platelet Agglutination (LD-RIPA).
    • Type 2M: Loss-of-function mutations in the A1 domain impairing GPIb binding without disturbing multimer assembly. Results: Low vWF:Activity, normal vWF:Ag, Ratio $< 0.7$, but multimer analysis reveals all multimer sizes present.
    • Type 2N (Normandy): Mutations in the FVIII-binding region (D'/D3 domains). Platelet adhesion is normal (normal vWF:Ag, normal vWF:Act, Ratio $\ge 0.7$), but circulating Factor VIII cannot bind and is cleared rapidly. Factor VIII:C is markedly reduced ($< 5\text{--}25\text{%}$), mimicking mild Hemophilia A.
  • Type 3 (Severe Quantitative Deficiency): Autosomal recessive complete gene deletion or nonsense mutation. vWF:Ag $< 5\text{ IU/dL}$ (often undetectable), vWF:Act undetectable, FVIII:C $< 10\text{%}$. Clinically presents with combined severe mucocutaneous and deep hemarthrotic bleeding.

Pre-Analytical Pitfalls

vWF is an acute-phase reactant synthesized and released under stress, intense physical exertion, acute infection, inflammation, pregnancy, or estrogen therapy. These states can elevate a baseline deficient level into the normal range, masking mild Type 1 vWD. If initial panel results are borderline normal in a patient with a strong personal or familial bleeding history, testing should be repeated when the patient is at baseline.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • An Activity:Antigen ratio $< 0.7$ identifies a qualitative Type 2 defect; a ratio $\ge 0.7$ with uniformly decreased parameters confirms quantitative Type 1.
  • Type 2B is distinct among vWD types for causing mild, intermittent thrombocytopenia due to in vivo clearing of hyper-adhesive platelet-vWF complexes.
  • Differentiating Type 2B vWD from Platelet-Type (Pseudo) vWD: in Type 2B, the molecular mutation is in the plasma vWF molecule; in Pseudo-vWD, the gain-of-function mutation resides in the patient's platelet GPIb receptor.
  • Type 2N presents with normal vWF levels and isolated low Factor VIII; it must be differentiated from Hemophilia A by evaluating Factor VIII-vWF binding affinity or genetic testing.

Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA) Principles and Agonist Pathways

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA), introduced by Born and O'Brien, remains the reference method for diagnosing functional platelet abnormalities.

Analytical Principle

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is turbid. When suspended in a cylindrical cuvette under constant magnetic stirring ($1,000\text{ rpm}$) at $37^\circ\text{C}$, the addition of an activating biochemical agonist initiates a series of platelet responses:

  1. Shape Change: Discoid platelets convert into spherical forms with pseudopodia, causing an immediate, transient dip in light transmittance.
  2. Primary Wave Aggregation: Agonist-receptor binding activates surface GPIIb/IIIa receptors, which bind fibrinogen bridges, producing reversible micro-aggregates.
  3. Secondary Wave Aggregation: Intracellular signaling cascades induce dense granules to secrete endogenous ADP, serotonin, and activate membrane phospholipase $\text{A}_2$, yielding thromboxane $\text{A}_2$ ($\text{TXA}_2$). This autocrine/paracrine loop drives irreversible, full-scale platelet clumping.
    As macro-aggregates form and precipitate from the suspension, optical clearance occurs, allowing more light to traverse the cuvette and hit the photodiode detector. Aggregation curves are plotted as percent aggregation (transmittance) versus time.

Standard Agonist Profiles & Molecular Pathways

  • Adenosine Diphosphate (ADP, $2\text{--}10\text{ }\mu\text{M}$):
    • Receptors: $\text{P2Y}_1$ (initiates calcium mobilization and shape change) and $\text{P2Y}_{12}$ (inhibits adenylyl cyclase, driving amplification and sustained GPIIb/IIIa activation).
    • Tracing: Produces a classic biphasic curve. The primary wave reflects external ADP binding; the secondary wave represents dense-granule ADP release.
  • Epinephrine ($5\text{--}10\text{ }\mu\text{M}$):
    • Receptors: $\alpha_{2\text{A}}$-adrenergic receptors coupled to $\text{G}_{\text{z}}$ proteins.
    • Tracing: Produces a biphasic response without an initial shape-change deflection. Epinephrine requires an intact COX-1/thromboxane pathway to trigger dense granule release; its secondary wave is abolished by aspirin.
  • Collagen ($1\text{--}5\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$):
    • Receptors: Integrin $\alpha_2\beta_1$ (primary adhesion) and Glycoprotein VI (GPVI, primary signaling receptor).
    • Tracing: Strong agonist producing a single, irreversible wave following a distinct $30\text{--}60\text{ second}$ lag phase during which platelets adhere, release contents, and synthesize $\text{TXA}_2$.
  • Arachidonic Acid (AA, $0.5\text{--}1.5\text{ mM}$):
    • Mechanism: Free fatty acid substrate acted upon sequentially by platelet cyclooxygenase-1 (yielding prostaglandin $\text{G}_2/\text{H}_2$) and thromboxane synthase to generate thromboxane $\text{A}_2$.
    • Tracing: Strong agonist producing a single, robust wave of irreversible aggregation with no preceding lag phase. If COX-1 is blocked, no aggregation occurs.
  • Ristocetin ($1.2\text{--}1.5\text{ mg/mL}$):
    • Mechanism: Non-physiological polycationic antibiotic that alters the molecular charge of vWF and the platelet GPIb/IX/V receptor complex, inducing vWF binding and agglutination.
    • Tracing: Single wave. This is true agglutination; it does not require metabolic platelet activation, functioning even with formalin-fixed platelets.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

Procedural and Pre-Analytical Protocol

  1. Medication Restrictions: Patients must abstain from aspirin, aspirin-containing compounds, and NSAIDs for at least $7\text{--}10\text{ days}$ prior to collection (reflecting the lifetime of the circulating platelet pool, as aspirin inhibits COX-1 irreversibly). Other anti-platelet therapies (e.g., clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor) require a 5- to 14-day clearance window under medical supervision.
  2. Specimen Collection: Atraumatic venipuncture using a 19- to 21-gauge needle into $3.2\text{%}$ sodium citrate. Evacuate a discard tube first if using a butterfly needle. Hemolysis activates platelets in vitro via released RBC ADP.
  3. Specimen Centrifugation:
    • Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP): Centrifuge whole blood at $150\text{--}200 \times g$ for $10\text{--}15\text{ minutes}$ at room temperature ($20\text{--}25^\circ\text{C}$). Remove the supernatant PRP using plastic transfer pipettes, avoiding the buffy coat. Target PRP platelet count: $200\text{--}300 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$.
    • Platelet-Poor Plasma (PPP): Centrifuge the remaining blood at $> 1,500 \times g$ for $15\text{ minutes}$. PPP must have a platelet count $< 10 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$.
  4. Instrument Setup and Calibration:
    • Maintain the aggregometer heating block at $37.0 \pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$.
    • Pipette patient PRP into a cuvette with a siliconized stir bar; set optical baseline to $0\text{%}$ aggregation (or $0\text{%}$ transmission).
    • Pipette patient PPP into a matching cuvette; set to $100\text{%}$ aggregation (cleared baseline).
    • All testing must be finalized within $4\text{ hours}$ of the original venipuncture.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Never store whole blood or PRP on wet ice or in a refrigerator; hypothermia alters the platelet membrane skeleton, stimulating spontaneous activation.
  • Biphasic curves (ADP, Epinephrine) reflect primary aggregation followed by secondary release of storage granules.
  • Collagen produces a characteristic lag phase of up to a minute while platelets undergo initial adhesion before releasing endogenous granules.
  • Arachidonic acid evaluates the direct integrity of the cyclooxygenase pathway: zero aggregation signifies complete enzyme inhibition.

Diagnostic Pathophysiology and Aggregation Profiles

  1. Glanzmann Thrombasthenia (GT):
    • Genetic Defect: Autosomal recessive mutations in ITGA2B or ITGB3 genes encoding the integrin subunit $\alpha_{\text{IIb}}\beta_3$ (Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa).
    • Pathophysiology: Platelets possess functional adhesion receptors (GPIb) and intact granule machinery, but lack the fibrinogen receptor required to bridge platelets together.
    • LTA Profile: Absent aggregation in response to all concentrations of ADP, Epinephrine, Collagen, and Arachidonic Acid. The response to Ristocetin remains completely normal because ristocetin-mediated agglutination depends on vWF and GPIb, bypassing GPIIb/IIIa.
  2. Bernard-Soulier Syndrome (BSS):
    • Genetic Defect: Autosomal recessive deficiency or absence of the Glycoprotein Ib/IX/V complex (GP1BA, GP1BB, GP9).
    • Pathophysiology: Platelets cannot bind vWF under physiological shear.
    • LTA Profile: Normal shape change, primary, and secondary aggregation in response to ADP, Epinephrine, Collagen, and Arachidonic Acid. Complete absence of agglutination with Ristocetin.
    • Distinguishing Feature: Ristocetin-induced agglutination is not corrected by the addition of normal control plasma (in contrast to von Willebrand disease, where normal plasma restores the missing vWF).
  3. Dense ($\delta$) Granule Storage Pool Disease:
    • Pathophysiology: Deficiency in the number or content of dense granules (which contain non-metabolic ADP, ATP, calcium, pyrophosphate, and serotonin). Seen in isolation or within syndromes like Hermansky-Pudlak and Chediak-Higashi.
    • LTA Profile: Intact initial shape change and normal primary aggregation wave with low-dose ADP and Epinephrine, but complete absence of the secondary wave due to lack of stored ADP. Aggregation in response to Collagen is markedly decreased. Ristocetin agglutination is normal.
  4. Aspirin and Cyclooxygenase Pathway Inhibitors:
    • Pathophysiology: Aspirin irreversibly acetylates serine-529 of platelet COX-1, permanently preventing conversion of arachidonic acid to thromboxane $\text{A}_2$. NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen) produce reversible competitive inhibition.
    • LTA Profile: Complete, flat-line absence of aggregation with Arachidonic Acid. Epinephrine displays a normal primary wave with absent secondary wave. ADP at low concentrations shows an absent secondary wave; high concentrations of ADP may overcome this inhibition. Collagen aggregation is impaired at low concentrations but approaches normal at high concentrations. Ristocetin response is normal.

Diagnostic Confirmation Strategies

  • Platelet Flow Cytometry: If LTA indicates an absence of aggregation across standard physiological agonists (GT) or isolated ristocetin failure (BSS), flow cytometry provides definitive immunophenotypic confirmation:
    • Glanzmann Thrombasthenia: Loss or reduction of surface CD41 (integrin $\alpha_{\text{IIb}}$) and CD61 (integrin $\beta_3$).
    • Bernard-Soulier Syndrome: Loss or reduction of surface CD42b (GPIb$\alpha$), CD42a (GPIX), CD42c (GPIb$\beta$), and CD42d (GPV).
  • Electron Microscopy (EM): Whole mount or thin-section transmission electron microscopy is the diagnostic standard to quantify dense ($\delta$) granules per platelet (normally 4–8 per platelet) to confirm storage pool deficiency.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • The Glanzmann Rule: Everything is flat-line absent except Ristocetin.
  • The Bernard-Soulier Rule: Everything is completely normal except Ristocetin.
  • The vWD vs. BSS Ristocetin Mixing Differential: If ristocetin-induced agglutination fails, add normal plasma to the cuvette. If agglutination is restored, the defect is plasma vWF deficiency (vWD). If it remains absent, the defect is the platelet receptor (Bernard-Soulier).
  • The Aspirin Signature: Flat-line response to Arachidonic Acid combined with absent secondary waves for ADP and Epinephrine.

Primary Hemostasis Diagnostic Reflex Algorithm

Core Concepts & Detailed Procedure / Concept Analysis

Investigating primary hemostasis disorders requires an algorithmic approach to balance diagnostic yield, turn-around time, and financial stewardship. Because clinical presentations of primary hemostatic defects are uniform (epistaxis, menorrhagia, ecchymoses, gingival bleeding), the laboratory must differentiate quantitative defects from qualitative platelet disorders and von Willebrand disease.

Clinical Correlations, Diagnostics & QC

The rationale for the reflex progression:

  1. Rule out quantitative deficiencies first (CBC/PBS): It is clinically inappropriate and cost-ineffective to order a PFA or LTA on a severely thrombocytopenic patient. The CBC resolves the clinical question in the majority of bleeding presentations.
  2. Global functional screening (PFA-100/200): If the platelet count is adequate ($\ge 100 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) and hematocrit is $\ge 30\text{%}$, the PFA screens for platelet dysfunction and vWD under physiological shear.
  3. von Willebrand Disease Panel Reflex: Because vWD is the most common inherited bleeding diathesis (prevalence $\approx 1\text{%}$), a dual-prolonged PFA (CEPI and CADP prolonged) must trigger a vWD diagnostic panel before embarking on labor-intensive platelet aggregometry.
  4. Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA): If the vWD panel is entirely normal, testing progresses to LTA to interrogate the biochemical activation pathways of the platelet membrane and interior storage granules.
  5. Specialized Structural Assays (Flow Cytometry & Electron Microscopy): Serve as tertiary confirmatory tests to establish definitive genetic receptor absence or granule organelle depletion.

Key Takeaways & Diagnostic Pearls

  • Never proceed to functional primary hemostasis testing without first verifying that the platelet count is $\ge 100 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$ and the hematocrit is $\ge 30\text{%}$.
  • The classical screening cascade follows a tiered progression: CBC/Smear $\rightarrow$ PFA $\rightarrow$ vWD Panel $\rightarrow$ LTA $\rightarrow$ Flow Cytometry/EM.
  • An abnormal screening PFA with a normal vWD panel points to an intrinsic platelet defect, requiring Light Transmission Aggregometry.
  • Normal routine coagulation assays (PT and aPTT) do not rule out primary hemostatic disorders; with the exception of severe Type 3 or Type 2N vWD (which affect Factor VIII), primary hemostatic disorders typically yield normal PT and aPTT results.

ASCP MLS Content Guideline Alignment

Hematology

  • Physiology
    • Primary Hemostasis Mechanisms
      • Platelet ultrastructure, open canalicular system, dense tubular system, and organelle distribution
      • Endothelial cell interactions, subendothelial collagen binding, and von Willebrand factor bridging
      • Platelet kinetics, thrombopoietin regulation, endomitosis, and circulating lifespan (7–10 days)
      • Biochemical cascades: cyclooxygenase-1, thromboxane $\text{A}_2$, and granule exocytosis
  • Disease States
    • Thrombocytopenia and Quantitative Platelet Disorders
      • Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP): isolated thrombocytopenia, high MPV, compensatory megakaryopoiesis
      • Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (TTP, HUS, DIC): consumptive thrombocytopenia, fragmentation hemolysis, schistocyte identification
      • Bone marrow aplasia, myelodysplasia, and leukemic marrow replacement: hypoproliferative thrombocytopenia with low MPV
      • Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome: pathognomonic microthrombocytopenia (low PLT, low MPV)
    • Qualitative Platelet Disorders (Thrombocytopathies)
      • Glanzmann thrombasthenia: autosomal recessive deficiency of integrin $\alpha_{\text{IIb}}\beta_3$ (GPIIb/IIIa, CD41/CD61)
      • Bernard-Soulier syndrome: autosomal recessive absence of GPIb/IX/V complex (CD42b), giant platelets, thrombocytopenia
      • Platelet storage pool diseases: dense ($\delta$) granule deficiency, absent secondary aggregation wave
      • Acquired qualitative defects: drug-induced COX-1 inhibition (aspirin, NSAIDs) and uremic thrombopathy
    • Von Willebrand Disease (vWD)
      • Pathophysiological basis: defective primary hemostatic adhesion and secondary Factor VIII stabilization
      • Type 1: partial quantitative reduction, proportionate decrease in vWF:Ag, vWF:Act, and FVIII:C (Ratio $\ge 0.7$)
      • Type 2 variants: qualitative defects (2A, 2B, 2M, 2N) characterized by an Activity-to-Antigen ratio $< 0.7$
      • Type 2B hyper-adhesive gain-of-function variants and low-dose RIPA responsiveness
      • Type 2N FVIII-binding mutations mimicking Hemophilia A
      • Type 3: complete absence of vWF with secondary Factor VIII deficiency
  • Laboratory Testing
    • Routine Automated Platelet Analysis
      • Electronic impedance counting principles, aperture dynamics, and pulse height volume analysis
      • Optical flow cytometric scatter (low-angle FSC and orthogonal SSC) and RNA fluorescence quantification
      • Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) and Platelet Distribution Width (PDW) calculation and clinical interpretation
    • Platelet Function Analyzer (PFA-100/200)
      • High-shear in vitro capillary aperture closure time technology ($5,000\text{--}6,000\text{ s}^{-1}$)
      • Collagen/Epinephrine (CEPI) cartridge screening dynamics and aspirin sensitivity
      • Collagen/ADP (CADP) cartridge reflex testing and interpretation algorithms
      • Pre-analytical exclusion limits: validation of platelet count ($\ge 100 \times 10^3/\mu\text{L}$) and hematocrit ($\ge 30\text{%}$)
    • Von Willebrand Disease Diagnostic Assays
      • von Willebrand Factor Antigen (vWF:Ag) quantitative latex immunoassay turbidimetry
      • von Willebrand Factor Activity: classical Ristocetin Cofactor (vWF:RCo) and recombinant GPIbM methods
      • One-stage clot-based Factor VIII coagulant activity (modified aPTT) using factor-deficient substrate plasma
      • vWF Activity to vWF Antigen calculation and interpretive classification thresholds
      • Specialized secondary diagnostics: SDS-agarose gel multimer electrophoresis and RIPA
    • Light Transmission Aggregometry (LTA)
      • Born optical aggregometry methodology: PRP ($0\text{%}$) and PPP ($100\text{%}$) spectrophotometric calibration
      • Agonist profiling and pathway interrogation: ADP, Epinephrine, Collagen, Arachidonic Acid, Ristocetin
      • Biphasic curve analysis: primary aggregation vs. autocrine dense granule release secondary wave
      • Diagnostic tracing identification for Glanzmann thrombasthenia, Bernard-Soulier, storage pool defects, and aspirin
    • Specialized Confirmatory Modalities
      • Platelet surface flow cytometry immunophenotyping: CD41/CD61 (GPIIb/IIIa) and CD42b (GPIb)
      • Transmission electron microscopy for dense granule enumeration

Laboratory Operations

  • Pre-Analytical Specimen Assessment and Troubleshooting
    • Anticoagulant requirements: $3.2\text{%}$ sodium citrate ratio ($9:1$) and non-wettable plastic containers
    • EDTA-dependent pseudothrombocytopenia remediation, citrate collection, and $1.1$ dilution correction calculations
    • Recognition and resolution of platelet satellitism, red blood cell microcytosis, and schistocyte interference
    • Specimen stability windows, storage temperature restrictions ($15\text{--}25^\circ\text{C}$), and avoidance of hypothermic cold activation
    • Medication abstention protocols: antiplatelet drug clearance timelines prior to functional aggregometry testing
  • Quality Control & Regulatory Compliance
    • Analyzer calibration, Normal and Abnormal commercial controls, and run acceptance protocols for PFA and LTA systems
    • Verification of physiological baselines, acute-phase reactant elevations, and clinical repeat testing criteria for vWD panels
