Procedure

Microhematocrit (Packed Cell Volume)
Phase 1

Specimen Preparation & Centrifugation
  1. Mix & Fill: Thoroughly invert EDTA whole blood. Fill two microcapillary tubes approximately 2/3 to 3/4 full.
  2. Seal Tube: Wipe the exterior of each tube clean and seal one end with a 4 to 5 mm Critoseal clay plug.
  3. Centrifuge: Load tubes into the microhematocrit centrifuge with sealed ends facing outward against the rim cushion.
  4. Spin & Stop: Centrifuge at ~5,000 rpm (10,000–15,000 × g) for 5 minutes. Allow rotor to stop naturally without manual braking.
Phase 2

Reader Alignment & Measurement
  1. Set 0% Baseline: Place the tube in the holder with the clay / RBC interface aligned exactly on the black baseline (0% line).
  2. Set Bottom Dial to 100%: Set BOTTOM ACTIVE (TOP FIXED) and rotate the bottom disc until the 100% line aligns with the vertical tube slot (0°).
  3. Align Plasma Meniscus (100%): Set TOP ACTIVE (BOTTOM FIXED) and rotate the spiral line until it intersects the top of the plasma meniscus.
  4. Read at RBC Meniscus: Set BOTH LINKED and rotate both discs together until the spiral line intersects the top of the packed RBC column (RBC / buffy coat interface). Exclude the buffy coat layer.
  5. Record Percentage: Read the hematocrit percentage (%) directly from the bottom scale at the alignment index line.
Phase 3

Duplicate Verification & Reporting
  1. Verify Duplicate Agreement: Read the second duplicate tube. Results must agree within ±1.0% (≤1.0% difference). If >1.0%, repeat in duplicate.
  2. Average & Report: Average the acceptable duplicate values and report the final hematocrit to the nearest 0.1% (or per laboratory protocol).
Critical Reminders & Error Sources
  • Buffy Coat: Never include the white buffy coat (WBCs/platelets) in the reading; inclusion causes falsely elevated results.
  • Seal Integrity: Poor or loose clay sealing allows RBC leakage during spinning, resulting in falsely decreased values.
  • Under-Centrifugation: Inadequate spin speed/time causes incomplete packing and trapped plasma, leading to falsely elevated results.
  • Trapped Plasma: Manual spun hematocrit is typically 1% to 3% higher than automated analyzer HCT due to minute trapped plasma between cells.
Quick Memory Aid
